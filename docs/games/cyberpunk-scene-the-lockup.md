# The Lockup — a playable scene for Cyberpunk Red

*Text-only. No engine required. Read it, make the choices, roll when it says roll.*

**System:** Cyberpunk Red. **Players:** 1-4. **Runtime:** 20-40 minutes.
**Setup:** Each player has a character with the nine CPR stats (INT, REF, COOL, TECH, LUCK, ATT, MA, EMP, BODY) and a handful of skills at 2-6. Roll 1d10 + STAT + SKILL against the DV shown. A natural 10 explodes (roll again, add). A natural 1 is a fumble: the situation gets worse, not just failed.

---

## The job

Kestrel, a fixer with a voice like a filing cabinet, gives you the address in eleven words:

> "Container 44, Watson lockup, Bay Nine. Case is cryo-sealed. Do not open it."

Twenty thousand eddies, half up front. She does not say what is in the case. She does not say who else wants it. She pays in clean eddies, which in Night City is its own kind of warning: clean money means someone upstream is very careful.

The lockup is a fenced yard of stacked shipping containers under the wreck of the old Interstate, three blocks from where the Combat Zone starts meaning it. Sodium lights, chain-link, a single prefab office with a light on. Rain coming down like static.

**Do you scout first, or go in fast?**

- **A. Scout.** Spend twenty minutes on the fence line reading the yard. → *The Fence Line*
- **B. Go in fast.** Half up front means the clock is already running. → *Bay Nine*

---

## The Fence Line

You find a gap in the chain-link where someone else already cut it, and re-twisted it badly. Amateur work, done recently. The wire is still bright at the cut.

Two things become clear from the dark:

1. Container 44 sits at the bottom of a stack, third from the left. Its door seal is intact, but the padlock is new — and there is a second lock, a matte-black cylinder, wired into the container's own power. That is not a lockup lock. That is somebody's property.
2. There is a man in the prefab office who is not the night guard. He is sitting too still, and the guard's coffee is on the step outside, going cold in the rain.

**Roll COOL + Perception, DV 13.**
- **Success:** you catch the second man — a woman, actually, in the dark behind the stacked empties on the east side. She has a long case and no flashlight, which means she already knows the yard.
- **Fail:** you see the office light click off. You have maybe ninety seconds before the yard stops being yours.

Either way, you can now:

- **A1. Talk to the woman.** She is another runner on the same job. → *Two Runners*
- **A2. Ignore her, go straight for the lock.** → *Bay Nine*

---

## Two Runners

She does not draw. That is the first interesting thing about her. The second is that she is holding a slate with the container manifest already pulled up — she has been here longer than you and she still has not opened the door, which means the door is the problem.

"Kestrel sold it twice," she says. "Or her client did. Either way we're both being paid to be someone's deniable asset."

She offers a split: she takes the case to her buyer, you get paid to make sure nobody follows her, and she tells you what she knows about the black cylinder.

- **A1a. Take the deal.** → *The Cylinder*
- **A1b. Refuse and take the case yourself.** She will not fight you here — too much noise — but you have made an enemy who knows your face. → *Bay Nine* (add 1 to the Complication clock)
- **A1c. Ask what is in the case.** She laughs. "Kestrel said don't open it. She didn't say don't ask." → *What Is In The Case*

---

## What Is In The Case

She shows you the manifest photo on the slate. One line is redacted. One line is not:

> `CONTENTS: 1x cryo unit, medical, non-transferable. HANDLER: [REDACTED]. DESTINATION: Biotechnica Reclamation, Night City.`

Medical cryo. Non-transferable. A destination that is a corporate reclamation facility, which is the polite word for where things go when a company wants them back and does not want a court to see them.

Nobody paid twenty thousand eddies for a cooler. Somebody paid it to keep a person out of a building.

**This is the moment the scene turns.** Whatever you do next, say out loud what your character believes about the case. Not what is profitable. What is true.

- **A1c-i. Deliver it anyway.** The eddies are real, the person in the box is a problem you were not hired to have. → *Bay Nine*
- **A1c-ii. Take the case and go dark.** You will not deliver it to Biotechnica. You will also not get the other half. → *Bay Nine* (the woman will help you, for her own reasons)

---

## The Cylinder

She knows three things about the matte-black cylinder, and two of them are useful.

1. It is a **dead-man lock**. Cut the power and it opens. Cut the power wrong and it also opens — into the container's own interior alarm loop, which calls the yard's owner, not the cops.
2. It was installed with a **service override** because installation crews are lazy. The override is a physical key, and installers leave keys where installers always leave keys.
3. She does not know where.

**Roll TECH + Security Tech, DV 15.** Or **INT + Deduction, DV 17** to reason it out instead of defeating it.

- **Success:** the override is a brass key on a nail inside the prefab office, behind the door, at shoulder height, exactly where a tired installer would hang it. Getting it means going into the office with the too-still man in it.
- **Fail:** you spend four minutes on the cylinder and learn only that it is better made than anything else in this yard.

---

## Bay Nine

The yard opens up in front of you like a mouth. Rain on metal, a hundred small sounds pretending to be footsteps.

Container 44 is where they said. The door is cold enough that the rain steams off the top edge, which is wrong — cryo units leak heat, not cold. This one is running cold on the *outside*, which means it is not a cooler. It is a container-sized heat sink, and it has been working hard.

**Three things are true at once.** Pick the one your character deals with first:

- **The lock.** The black cylinder is live and humming. → *The Cylinder*
- **The office.** The too-still man has not moved in six minutes, and that is longer than a man sits still. → *The Office*
- **The other runner.** She is behind you, and she has stopped pretending not to be. → *Two Runners*

**Then roll INITIATIVE.** Whoever is still standing in the yard when it resolves gets the case.

---

## The Office

Up close, the too-still man is not still because he is calm. He is still because he is dead, and he has been dead long enough for the coffee outside to go cold. No wounds. A patch on his neck, faded, pharmaceutical-gray.

The office has: a chair, a thermos, a manifest binder, a brass key on a nail, and a phone with one number in its history, dialed four times in the last hour.

The last call connected. **Somebody knows the yard is occupied.** You have minutes, not hours.

**Roll INT + Criminology, DV 12** to read the binder: container 44 was re-designated six days ago from `HOLD - DISPUTED` to `TRANSFER - PRIORITY`. Someone paid to move it up the schedule. That is not a rival runner. That is the owner.

---

## Complication clock (GM track)

Tick one box at each of these: a fumble, a refused deal, a failed cylinder roll, a shot fired, or ten minutes of table time spent arguing.

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

At **3**, a second vehicle enters the yard. At **6**, the yard owner arrives personally, and the scene becomes a chase, not a retrieval. The case does not matter anymore. What your character decided about the case matters.

---

## Endings

- **The Delivery.** The case goes to Biotechnica. You get paid. You do not sleep well. Somewhere in a reclamation facility, a cryo unit is signed for by someone whose name is not on any manifest.
- **The Dark.** You take the case and go. You have a person in a box and no buyer. This is the beginning of a longer story, not the end of this one.
- **The Burn.** You open it in the yard, in the rain, because you decided the person inside outranks the job. Everything after this is a different game.
- **The Nothing.** The clock runs out. The yard owner takes the case, and you never learn what was in it, and that is the ending most runners actually get.
